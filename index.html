COMPLETE THEORY

Everything you need to know

1. Motion

Motion means a change in the position of an object with time relative to a reference point. A moving car, a flying bird and a person walking are examples of motion.

2. Rest

An object is at rest when its position does not change with time relative to a chosen reference point. Rest and motion are relative to the observer/reference point.

3. Reference point

To decide whether something has moved, we compare its position with a reference point such as a tree, building or pole.

4. Types of motion

Rectilinear: along a straight line. Circular: along a circular path. Rotational: spinning about an axis. Periodic: repeating after equal intervals.

5. Distance

Distance is the total length of the path travelled. Its SI unit is metre (m). Larger distances are commonly measured in kilometres (km).

6. Time

The SI unit of time is second (s). Minute and hour are commonly used units. 1 minute = 60 seconds and 1 hour = 3600 seconds.

7. Speed

Speed tells how fast an object moves. It is the distance travelled in unit time. SI unit: metre per second (m/s). Another common unit is km/h.

8. Uniform motion

An object has uniform motion when it covers equal distances in equal intervals of time. Its speed remains constant.

9. Non-uniform motion

If an object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time, or its speed changes, the motion is non-uniform.

10. Measuring time

People have used natural repeating events to measure time. Modern clocks use regular repeating processes. A pendulum is an example of periodic motion used in traditional clocks.

11. Simple pendulum

A simple pendulum has a bob suspended by a thread from a fixed support. One complete to-and-fro movement is one oscillation. The time for one oscillation is its time period.

12. Distance–time graph

Time is normally on the horizontal axis and distance on the vertical axis. A straight sloping line represents uniform motion; a horizontal line represents rest.

FORMULA SHEET

Remember these

Speed

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Example: 100 m in 20 s → 100 ÷ 20 = 5 m/s

Distance

Distance = Speed × Time

Example: 6 m/s for 10 s → 6 × 10 = 60 m

Time

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Example: 200 m at 8 m/s → 200 ÷ 8 = 25 s

Conversions

1 m/s = 3.6 km/h

1 km/h = 5/18 m/s
1 km = 1000 m
1 h = 3600 s
